Issue 02 · 15 Nov 2026 · Covers 12 to 14 November 2026

The story is the asset

A quiet week between Geneva and New York, with thirteen sales over the line. The best prices went to objects with a famous past owner. The worst went to the biggest stone, because its catalogue entry admitted a doubt.

Top line

This was the gap between Geneva's jewels week and the New York evening sales, which start on Monday. Thirteen sales made the cut. Seven are Sotheby's Geneva jewels from 11 November, whose results came out after the last issue closed.

Past owners drove the best prices. The Romanov Sapphire, worn by a Russian empress, made CHF 4.12 million against a high estimate of CHF 1.7 million, per NBC News. A brooch from two Swedish princesses made almost 19 times its high estimate, per Royal Watcher.

In Shanghai, Christie's sold every lot in its evening sale for CNY 79.5 million, its results page shows. The biggest stone of the week, a 90.80-carat diamond necklace, sold below its low estimate. In Dallas, a Darth Vader lightsaber made $4.875 million, a record for Star Wars, Heritage says.

Who bought

One of 13 buyers is on the record.

  • Moussaieff Jewellers bought a 51.79-carat unheated spinel for CHF 1.66 million, more than three times its high estimate, National Jeweler reports, citing Sotheby's. Moussaieff is a London family firm that started in the pearl and gem trade in Bukhara in the 1850s, per Solitaire Magazine. It is a dealer, so it bought to sell again.

The other twelve are unnamed. What is on the record:

  • Romanov Sapphire: a private collector, per AP via NBC News.
  • 8.63-carat pink diamond: a private buyer, per Rapaport.
  • Swedish royal brooch: two phone bidders fought for it, per Royal Watcher.
  • Picasso in Shanghai: a telephone bidder, per Yicai.

One update from last issue. The buyer of the Bulgari Pink is still unnamed, but Christie's head of jewellery told the dpa news agency the ring was bought as a gift, to be worn, per La Sicilia.

Market signal

A famous past owner was worth more this week than a better stone. A study by ALT and FNDATA, reported by the Financial Times, found royal and noble jewels sell for a median 2.3 times their presale estimate, against 1.9 times for jewels from other public figures. This week fits. The Romanov bracelet made 2.4 times its high estimate. A Cartier opal tiara worn at the 1953 coronation beat its high by about half.

The best counter-example was in the same Geneva room. A Graff blue diamond ring was offered with no reserve, meaning the seller agreed in advance to take the top bid, whatever it was. It sold below its low estimate even with fees, per Rapaport. It had no famous owner, and nobody paid extra.

Opinion: the Shanghai necklace is the more useful lesson for a buyer. Christie's catalogue says its centre stone is graded light brown and that the coloured diamonds had not been tested for natural colour. It sold for CNY 8.32 million against a low estimate of CNY 8.6 million. Buyers pay more for a history they can verify and less for a stone that is missing a test certificate. If you sell, get the paperwork done first.

Fine art was quieter. In Shanghai, Chen Danqing's Shepherd's Call made CNY 4.22 million against a high estimate of CNY 600,000, Christie's results show. It came from an American collector who bought it in New York in 1982 and was offered for the first time. A late Zao Wou-Ki sold inside its range but, if ARTSK has the 2019 price right, for about a quarter less than the seller paid.

Outside fine art

  • Jewelry: eight of this week's thirteen rows. Two unmounted pink diamonds show how colour grade sets price: 8.63 carats in Fancy Intense made CHF 4.49 million, and 8.11 carats one grade paler made CHF 1.56 million, per Il Sole 24 Ore.
  • Memorabilia: the Vader lightsaber broke the Star Wars record for the second time since July, when a Luke Skywalker lightsaber made $3.75 million, ARTnews reports.
  • Watches, cars, design: nothing over the line in the window. Phillips and Christie's Hong Kong watch sales run from 21 November.

How to read these numbers

Three terms that came up this week.

  • Estimate is the house's guess at the hammer price, set with the seller before the sale. It leaves out the fee. The seller's secret minimum, called the reserve, usually sits at or below the low estimate.
  • No reserve means the seller set no minimum. The lot sells to the highest bid. The price is whatever bidders were willing to pay, with nothing propping it up.
  • Type IIa is a lab grade for diamonds with almost no nitrogen. They are rare, often very clear, and buyers pay more for them, as with the 8.63-carat pink this week.
