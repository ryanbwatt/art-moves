# Art Moves: pressure test

14 Nov 2026. An audit of the project against Tom Bilyeu's public validation rules, followed by the questions the editor needs to answer. The editor's answers are at the end.

## The rules used

| Rule | Bilyeu's wording | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Attack the idea | "'Why will this work?' finds hope. 'Why will this fail?' finds truth." | [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DS2qyxklg62/) |
| Five tests | Problem verification, market size, competitor assessment, zero-cost test, early adopter interviews. Each ends "= dead idea" if it fails. | [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/DIW4Q1Ov8B3/) |
| Name the buyer, the result, the channel, the math | "If 1,000 people won't pay you $100, you don't have a business. You have an expensive hobby with a website." | [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQ5Fedfji_i/) |
| Test before building | "Stop protecting your idea from reality. Start testing it." | [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/tombilyeu/) |
| Keep score | "Companies that measure nothing improve nothing." | [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/tombilyeu/) |

Art Moves is not meant to make money, so "pay $100" is read as "do something that costs effort": open it unprompted, forward it, go to a show, buy a first work.

## Audit

Call: **change it, do not kill it.** The build is ahead of the proof. Two sessions have proposed six features (way-in pyramid, guess-the-price card, picks, read-through, reader questions, a why-buy page) for a site with no confirmed reader.

| Test | Finding | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Is the pain real | Beginners do ask how to start, and the standard advice matches the thesis: begin with signed prints and "disabuse any notion that you will make money" ([r/artcollecting](https://www.reddit.com/r/artcollecting/comments/1e201v8/beginner_looking_to_get_into_art_collecting/)). For the target reader the only evidence is Matt, reported second-hand. | Unproven for this reader |
| Is it big enough | In 2022, 16% of US men visited an art museum or gallery against 20% of women ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/b1-report-202403.pdf)). The gap is real and small. The most common barrier to arts participation is lack of time ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/Why-We-Engage-08.2022.pdf)), not lack of interest. | The premise is weaker than assumed; no target number set |
| Is there an advantage | Insider market coverage is crowded. Wall Power (formerly Artelligence) had 32,000 subscribers when Puck bought it and already reaches bankers ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/04/09/puck-acquires-substack-newsletter-artelligence)). The Gray Market covers art from the finance and tech side ([Substack](https://thegraymarket.substack.com/about)). Art & Object claims 50,000 general readers ([Art & Object](https://www.artandobject.com/subscribe)). None found pairs a trophy sale with a way in at $5,000 to $20,000 for a finance reader. The search was not exhaustive. | A gap, not yet a proven want |
| Tested before building | The site has no subscribe form, no feed and no visit counting. As of 12 Nov the first reader had not seen Issue 1. | The largest hole |
| Does the math work | There is no number for readers, for visits, or for what counts as working. | Missing |

## Questions for the editor

Each has a lean. "Agree" is a valid answer.

**The reader**

1. What has stopped Matt from buying so far: not knowing what he likes, fear of overpaying, not knowing where to go, or time? Lean: ask him, do not guess.
2. Name five men besides Matt who would read this. Lean: those five and Matt are the test group for six issues.

**The result**

3. What has changed for one reader after six months? Lean: a ladder of reads it, goes to see something, buys. Count the middle rung, because buying will be rare.
4. Is this a publication, or you advising friends with a site attached? Lean: the site gives routes and views; personal advice stays private.

**Reach and score**

5. How does a reader learn Issue 2 exists? Lean: a Sunday email and simple visit counts. Both need approval as new uses of a service.
6. Which three numbers go on the scoreboard? Lean: issues opened unprompted, forwards or replies, and a public record of picks (estimate, call, result).
7. What result would make you change or stop? Lean: after six issues, fewer than three of the six test readers open it without a nudge.

**Why you**

8. What do you have that Wall Power and The Gray Market do not, and does keeping your name off the site weaken it? Lean: translation for finance readers plus the way in; revisit the name when views on what to buy ship.
9. How long can the Sunday edit take for a year? Lean: 60 minutes. Anything that needs more comes out of the format.

**Credibility**

10. In one sentence, why lead with price if return is a poor reason to buy?
11. Will Art Moves ever point to an artist your gallery shows? Lean: no for the first twelve issues, then only with a label.
12. Did you spot-check the seven featured sales in Issue 1? Lean: do it this week and log what you find.

**This week**

13. Will you send Issue 1 to the first reader by Saturday and ask for three blunt reactions? Lean: yes. It turns the weakest answer into the strongest.
14. Freeze features for now, or prototype? Lean: freeze. Let Sunday's run and a reader's reaction come first. This reverses the earlier recommendation to prototype the read-through first.

Still open from earlier sessions: image policy, view wording (Worth a look / Wait / Pass).
