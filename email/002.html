The story is the asset

Plus, a dealer outbidding collectors for a spinel, and a Darth Vader lightsaber that set a record.

1. From the editor's desk

Past owners set the prices this week. A Romanov sapphire that has been to auction three times before made 2.4 times its high estimate, and a brooch from two Swedish princesses made almost 19 times. In Shanghai, the week's largest diamond sold below its low estimate after Christie's noted that its coloured stones had not been tested. Buyers pay up for a known past and mark down anything they cannot verify.

2. The number

18.7 times. What a padparadscha sapphire brooch made against its high estimate at Sotheby's Geneva: CHF 1.12 million against CHF 60,000. The stone came from two Swedish princesses. Without that name, it was priced like a good brooch.

3. Three sales

Cartier, The Romanov Sapphire, 197.80-carat unheated Ceylon sapphire set in a bracelet
CHF 4.12M ($4.97M) · Sotheby's Geneva, 11 Nov 2026
A stone with three earlier auction appearances made 2.4 times its high estimate.

Lucasfilm production prop, Darth Vader hero dueling lightsaber, screen-matched to The Empire Strikes Back and Return of the Jedi
$4.88M · Heritage Auctions Dallas, 12 Nov 2026
A film prop beat every Star Wars object ever sold, and the record has been broken twice in four months.

William Goldberg, 'Starlight' coloured diamond and diamond necklace, centred on a 90.80-carat pear-shaped diamond
CNY 8.32M ($1.25M) · Christie's Shanghai, 13 Nov 2026
The biggest stone of the week sold below its low estimate even with fees.

4. Who bought

One of 13 buyers is on the record.

Moussaieff Jewellers: Unsigned, Unmounted 51.79-carat unheated Tanzanian spinel, CHF 1.66M ($2.01M). National Jeweler, citing Sotheby's.

The other 12 are anonymous. Art Moves does not print guesses about private people.

5. One term

Provenance. The record of who owned an object, and when. In a market with no earnings or cash flow, a famous past owner is the closest thing to a brand, and buyers pay for it like one.
